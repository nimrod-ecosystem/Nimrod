# About Nimrod (context for a setup assistant)

**What it is:** a free, open-source, privacy-first platform that helps people with TBI,
stroke, and other serious conditions live better and recover — and gives their families and
caregivers real tools. The first product on it is **Cici**, a local, private care companion.

**How to think about it:**
- Everyone gets a **profile** (their outward face — how others reach them) and a
  **dashboard** (their own screen, built from modules).
- **Modules** are the building blocks: photos, camera (a rearview mirror), video calls,
  music/YouTube, sing-along, communication (AAC) boards, games, and more.
- It runs in a normal web browser on a phone, tablet, computer, or a small Raspberry Pi —
  no special hardware.

**Privacy (the core promise):**
- A patient's private information — camera, recordings, health data — stays on their own
  machine. The site connects people; it never becomes the middleman for private data.
- Video calls go directly between devices. The camera stays on the patient's device.
- Any AI that touches private data runs locally, on the user's own computer.

**What's live today:** a bedside dashboard (photos, self-view camera, YouTube + sing-along,
clock) and a tested library of accessible controls (yes/no scanning, switch/tilt,
press-to-play, hand and color tracking) plus calls, AAC, and games.

**What's planned:** accounts and profiles, a shareable module library, device-independent
setups, integrations (Home Assistant, Alexa, Google Home), letting a patient control their
own lights/thermostat through whatever input they can, and a private on-device companion.

**Tone to convey:** this exists to help real people and their families. It's careful,
gentle, and honest about what it is and isn't.

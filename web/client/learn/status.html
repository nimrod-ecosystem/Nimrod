Honest, on purpose

Built. Planned. Hoped.

Every claim on this site fits one of three buckets, and this page says which. "Built" means you can click it right now. "Planned" is real work that isn't done. "Hoped" is an idea nobody has committed to yet.

Built

  • Five course pages, each with a short and long outline, documentation, and a live "try it" link into the real software.
  • A proposed program calendar as an editable content pack.
  • The library dashboard: a real page you can set as an old device's home screen — clock, rotating quote and fact, one-tap games.
  • Cleveland Museum of Art Open Access images, downloaded and verified CC0, stored locally so the demo works offline.
  • A sample kiosk demo (Pictures, live view, Word Forge, Clock) that never shows a raw server error — it waits and retries instead.
  • Homeschool and Print Your Own pages (the latter's actual parts are still in design — see below).

Planned

  • AAC courses beyond the introductory one — deeper vocabulary-building sessions.
  • An opt-in group chat tied to class events.
  • Upload-your-own AAC boards, and using a board to control other things in the room.
  • Scan-and-select bingo, played entirely by switch-scanning.
  • A real Programs module reading the library's own calendar feed, replacing the hand-edited listing.
  • The three physical parts on Print Your Own (bookends, bookshelf, book-carrying case) — designed, not yet built.

Hoped

  • A private, local AI running on the machine in front of you — Course D's whole premise, not yet real.
  • A resources page of vetted 3D-printing and open-source AAC/AT links.
  • Calibration that reads a switch's own timing data back into a suggested setting, instead of hand-tuning.

How this was made

AI-assisted, with a person making every decision.

This site — and the Nimrod platform it's built on — was built with AI assistance (Claude), with Mike making the design decisions and the reasons behind them, not the AI. He is also building a local, private AI of his own, which is exactly what Course D on this site describes.

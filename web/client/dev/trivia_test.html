trivia

The score is about knowing things. It is never about how clearly somebody speaks.

This started as a speech-therapy game — say the word, be told how well you said it — and Mike killed it: put the score on the trivia instead, and you still know what word you were expecting. Better on both axes. Ethically, getting a trivia answer wrong is ordinary; being told your pronunciation was unclear is a verdict about your body on a screen you cannot walk away from. Technically, four choices is a four-way classification rather than open recognition, which is far easier and far more robust to atypical speech.

There is no recognizer yet and this does not pretend otherwise. What it does is mark the audio with what was asked and what was pressed — never with a claim about what was said aloud.

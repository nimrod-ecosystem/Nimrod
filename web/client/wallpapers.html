Something calm to watch

Where to find backgrounds, and what you can do with them today.

A screen that someone looks at all day should sometimes just be pleasant. These are places on the web where people share moving backgrounds and quiet pictures. This page also says plainly what can be put on a Nimrod screen right now.

Read this before you follow the links

You cannot yet paste one of these into a Nimrod screen. I would rather say so now than let you find out after an hour of looking.

Running code that somebody else wrote on a person’s screen means their program running next to that person’s photos and camera. That is a decision about safety I have not made yet. I will not make it quietly, just to add backgrounds.

Showing one straight from its own site has a second problem. It only works while the internet does. A screen beside somebody’s bed has to keep going when the wifi drops. And a site that stops to ask you to agree to something leaves the screen stopped, waiting for somebody to press a button. On a Nimrod screen, that is the one thing that must never happen.

So look around, get ideas, and tell me what you want. The calm things to watch that come with Nimrod are built in. They work with no internet and never wait for anybody. If you find something here you love, send it to me. Knowing what people want is what decides which ones get built next.

Moving pictures made by code (shaders and generative art)

They never end, never repeat, and are often beautiful. Good to rest your eyes on, such as flowing color, water or drifting specks of light.

Shadertoy

Thousands of full-screen moving pictures, each drawn by a small program called a shader. Most are slow-moving patterns. Search for “calm”, “water”, “clouds” or “nebula”. Runs in any modern browser.

License: each author chooses one for each piece. Many use Creative Commons (a family of licenses that let others reuse the work, each with its own rules). Plenty do not. Always check the page for the one you want.

p5.js examples

A friendly set of tools for drawing with code, with a large gallery made for teaching. The simplest place to start if you want to make something instead of finding it.

License: the examples have open licenses. They are made for learning from and changing.

three.js examples

The standard set of tools for 3D pictures in a browser. It has a huge official gallery of examples, such as water, drifting specks, landscapes and light.

License: three.js uses the MIT license (free to use, copy and change). Some examples include pictures or 3D models that have their own terms.

Sketches and experiments

Smaller and stranger. Their code is easier to read if you want to understand how something works.

CodePen

A very large collection of small web page experiments. Search for “ambient”, “particles”, “gradient” or “rain”. Quality varies a lot. The good ones are lovely.

License: each author sets one for each piece, and often none is stated. In practice that means all rights reserved (you may not reuse it). Ask the author before reusing anything.

OpenProcessing

A community that makes art with code, built around p5.js. People there share their work and build on each other’s. Its licenses are often more open than on CodePen.

License: many sketches have a stated Creative Commons license. It is shown on each sketch's page.

GLSL Sandbox

Simpler and older than Shadertoy. It is easier to take one effect from. Good for plain backgrounds of flat or swirling color.

License: largely unstated. Treat as all rights reserved.

Still images, if moving pictures are too much

For some people, movement is the wrong answer. It pulls at the eye of somebody who cannot look away. Still photos that change slowly are often kinder. You can do this today with the Photos panel and a folder of your own pictures.

Unsplash

A large collection of free, sharp photos. Landscapes and skies work well on a screen somebody looks at for hours.

License: free to use, including for business, without asking permission.

Wikimedia Commons

Millions of pictures with open licenses, including a great many nature and history photos.

License: open licenses, but most say you must credit the author. Each file’s page states its terms.

NASA image library

Earth, weather and deep space. Slow, enormous and calming. They also give a visitor who does not know what to say something to talk about.

License: mostly public domain (free for anyone to use). A few items have limits. Each page says.

None of the above is kept on Nimrod’s site, and none of it is Nimrod’s. These are other people’s communities and other people’s work. Check the license on anything you plan to reuse.

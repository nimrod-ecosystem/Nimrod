Something calm to watch

Where to find backgrounds, and what you can do with them today.

A screen that someone looks at all day should sometimes just be pleasant. These are the open communities worth browsing for animated backgrounds and quiet visuals — and a straight answer about what can be put on a bedside screen right now.

Read this before you follow the links

You cannot yet paste one of these into a Nimrod screen. We would rather say so than let you find out after an hour of looking.

Running code somebody else wrote on a screen in a care room means their JavaScript running next to that person's photos and camera. That is a decision about safety we have not made yet, and we are not going to make it quietly as a background feature.

Embedding one from its original site has a second problem: it only works while the internet does. A bedside screen has to keep going when the wifi drops, and an embed that stops to show a cookie banner is a screen that has stopped and is waiting for somebody to press something. For the person this was built for, that is the one thing that must never happen.

So: browse, get ideas, and tell us what you want. The calm visuals that ship with Nimrod are built in, work with no network, and never wait for anybody. If you find something here you love, send it to us — knowing what people actually want is what decides which ones get built next.

Shaders and generative art

Endless, non-repeating, and often genuinely beautiful. Good for something to rest your eyes on — flowing color, water, drifting particles.

Shadertoy

Thousands of full-screen animated shaders, most of them abstract and slow-moving. Search for “calm”, “water”, “clouds” or “nebula”. Runs in any modern browser.

License: per-item, chosen by each author. Many are Creative Commons; plenty are not. Always check the individual page.

p5.js examples

A friendly drawing library with a large teaching gallery. The simplest place to start if you want to make something rather than find it.

License: the examples are openly licensed and made for learning from and adapting.

three.js examples

The standard library for 3D in a browser, with a huge official example gallery — water, particles, landscapes, light.

License: three.js is MIT. Individual examples may bundle textures or models with their own terms.

Sketches and experiments

Smaller, weirder, and easier to read if you want to understand how something works.

CodePen

An enormous collection of small front-end experiments. Search for “ambient”, “particles”, “gradient” or “rain”. Quality varies wildly; the good ones are lovely.

License: per-pen, and often unstated — which in practice means all rights reserved. Ask the author before reusing anything.

OpenProcessing

A generative-art community built around p5.js, with a strong culture of sharing and remixing. Often more openly licensed than CodePen.

License: many sketches carry an explicit Creative Commons license. Shown on each sketch's page.

GLSL Sandbox

Simpler and older than Shadertoy, and easier to lift a single effect from. Good for plain color-field and plasma backgrounds.

License: largely unstated. Treat as all rights reserved.

Still images, if moving pictures are too much

For some people motion is the wrong answer entirely — it pulls at the eye of somebody who cannot look away. A slow rotation of still photographs is often kinder. You can do this today with the Photos panel and a folder of your own.

Unsplash

Large, free, high-resolution photography. Landscapes and skies work well on a screen somebody looks at for hours.

License: free to use, including commercially, without permission.

Wikimedia Commons

Millions of openly licensed images, including a great deal of nature and historical photography.

License: openly licensed, but most require attribution. Each file page states its terms.

NASA image library

Earth, weather and deep space. Slow, enormous, and calming — and a reliable conversation starter for a visitor who does not know what to say.

License: generally public domain. A few items are restricted; each page says.

Nimrod hosts none of the above and claims none of it — these are other people's communities and other people's work. Check the license on anything you intend to reuse.
